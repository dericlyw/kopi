# Owned: interview question bank

Ask one at a time. Pick what fits the topic. Each question hunts for a detail the model could never guess.

## Openers (pick one)
- What made you want to write about this now? What happened this week?
- Who is the one person you most want to read this, and what do you want them to do after?

## Lived detail
- When did you first run into this problem yourself? Where were you?
- What is a number you know from experience that most people would get wrong?
- Tell me about a time this went badly. What exactly went wrong?
- Who said something about this that stuck with you? What were their words?
- What did you believe about this two years ago that you no longer believe?

## Local and cultural texture
- What is different about this in your setting (your company, city, industry, culture) compared with the textbook version?
- Is there a local term, habit or rule that an outsider would not know?

## Stakes
- What happens if the reader ignores this?
- What does it cost, in money, time or face, to get this wrong?

## Follow-ups for vague answers
- "Can you give me the specific instance?"
- "What did that look like on the day?"
- "Roughly how many / how much / how long?"
- "What did you do next?"

## A fingerprint is good when
- It names something concrete (person, place, date, number, object, quote).
- It would be false if someone else wrote it.
- The author would be a little proud, or a little embarrassed, to see it in print.
